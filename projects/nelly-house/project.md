## Setting

Thirunelly, northern Wayanad. Paddy fields to the front, a coffee plantation behind, a tribal
settlement along one edge, the Brahmagiri Hills on the horizon. A stream runs through the
property. The house was sketched *around* the existing old trees rather than over them.

## Materials and construction

- **Rammed earth walls**, made from the site's own soil, excavated while carving a pond into the compound
- **Reclaimed terracotta roof tiles**, salvaged from buildings elsewhere
- **Reclaimed doors** approaching a hundred years old, from a heritage hospital being demolished in Kozhikode
- **Entrance door and windows** lifted from an old Kerala *mana* (traditional ancestral home)
- **Bamboo grown on the property**, used for sunscreens and gable detailing
- **Oxide flooring**, running unbroken from interior out onto the verandah
- Furniture almost entirely from the clients' own collection plus antiques sourced across Kerala
- Hanging lights by Akway, one of very few new elements
- Local tribal community involved in the build

## Quotes

> "We'd go up expecting to check on the construction, and end up on a nature walk instead, with a
> running commentary of fascinating facts about everything we saw."
> - **Ar. Thressia Paul**

> "Every opening in the mud wall had to be measured and executed with absolute precision" -
> the reclaimed frames came with their own stubborn dimensions, "leaving very little room for error."
> - **Ar. Faizan Hussain**

> "The remote location made logistics more demanding, particularly around sourcing materials and
> coordinating work."
> - **Ar. Thressia Paul**

> "We wanted a home that would blend with the natural rhythm of local life, where we could be
> peaceful together, and invite family and friends to share time with us."
> - **Karthika VK**, client

> "The verandah, inside yet outside. The birds, the squirrels, the grazing cows, the passing
> children - it's just the most peaceful time, sitting there and watching the world go by."
> - **Karthika VK**, client
